Almost every plot advertisement around Hyderabad carries one of two words: **HMDA** or **DTCP**. Which one applies to a plot is not a marketing detail. It decides who reviewed the layout, whether the plots in it can be registered by sale, and whether a building permission can be issued on them later.

Here is how the jurisdiction is decided, what a Gram Panchayat approval is actually worth, and how to check a specific layout yourself.

## Inside the Hyderabad Metropolitan Region, HMDA is the planning authority

The Hyderabad Metropolitan Development Authority is the planning authority for layouts inside the notified Hyderabad Metropolitan Region. That region has grown by government notification over the years, which is why a village that sat outside HMDA limits a decade ago can be inside them today — and why an outdated village list proves nothing.

It has been redrawn more than once, and most recently across 2025-26: G.O.Ms.No.68 of 12.03.2025 re-declared the Hyderabad Metropolitan Region, G.O.Ms.No.45 of 05.02.2026 extended Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation limits into the Core Urban Region with building-permission powers delegated to HMDA, and G.O.Ms.No.55 of 11.02.2026 constituted three municipal corporations. Read the notifications in force today against your village, and take the position to the authority that will sanction your plan rather than to a brochure.

Layout approval and land-use conversion run as separate tracks — a parcel can hold a layout approval without a conversion order, and the approval does not carry the conversion with it. The [land conversion guide](/blog/land-conversion-telangana-plots/) covers that second track.

Outside the notified region — and outside other notified development authorities — the Directorate of Town and Country Planning is the technical authority for layout approval, with the local municipality or Gram Panchayat handling what the rules delegate to it.

The practical test is not the district name on the brochure. It is the **revenue village, mandal and survey number** of the land, checked against the current notification.

## A Gram Panchayat approval is not a substitute for technical approval

Telangana's layout regularization scheme answers this question in its own FAQ, word for word:

> **Q. Whether a layout approved by a Gram Panchayat without technical approval of Director of Town & Country Planning/Urban Development Authority is a valid layout?**
> **No**

The same FAQ asks whether a Gram Panchayat layout that falls inside an urban development authority's limits without technical clearance — or inside a Master Plan area without DTCP approval — must be regularized. Both answers are **Yes**.

The consequences of leaving an unapproved layout unregularized are listed plainly:

- no building permission will be given;
- no water-supply connections, roads, drainage or street lighting will be extended;
- **no registration of such plots by sale will be permitted.**

That last line is the one buyers miss. An approval that was never issued cannot be fixed by a brochure, and a plot that cannot be registered is not yours.

## What the approval number tells you

Layout permissions are numbered, and the number points at the authority that issued it. Before paying a token on any plot:

1. **Ask for the approval number and the layout plan**, not the claim. "HMDA approved" on a hoarding is not evidence; the number is.
2. **Match the survey numbers, village and mandal** in the approval against the plot you are being shown. Layouts get split, renamed and sold plot-by-plot; the paper has to describe this land.
3. **Search the number on the issuing authority's portal.** HMDA publishes its planning and layout material, and Telangana's registration department publishes the list of prohibited properties that cannot be registered at all.
4. **If the project is RERA-registered, ask for the Form-C certificate and read the number** off it, then match the project name and location. The public search is captcha-gated and has no permalink that resolves a single number, so a failed lookup proves nothing either way. Registration and layout approval answer different questions; you need both, and the [RERA guide](/blog/rera-telangana-project-registration/) sets out what the number does and does not establish.
5. **Read the layout plan itself** — open space, roads, and any parcel marked mortgage or excluded from the saleable layout. A plot is only as good as its position on that plan.
6. **Ask a property lawyer to confirm the specific plot**, not just the layout. Individual plots can carry mortgages, easements or reservations that a layout approval does not address.

The [plot verification guide](/blog/verify-plot-hyderabad/) walks through the full document set — title, link documents, encumbrance and the plot-level checks.

The layout approval is the first approval, not the last one. Before you design a house, read [what building permission on a Telangana plot requires and who decides](/blog/building-permission-plot-telangana/), and take the sequence to the sanctioning authority.
