Buying a plot does not buy the right to build on it. The right to construct comes from a separate document — a building permit — granted by one specific authority after it has examined a plan for your plot. Until that document exists in your name, the plot is land, and construction on it is unauthorised no matter how clean the sale deed is.

This post sets out what the law now requires, who decides in your case, and the one fact that has changed recently enough to invalidate most guidance written before 2026. This is general buyer education, not legal advice.

## Who decides depends on exactly where the plot is

The answer lands in the first sentence: four different authorities can sanction a building plan in the Hyderabad area, and the plot's exact location picks one of them.

1. **GHMC**, the Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation, inside its own limits.
2. **HMDA**, the Hyderabad Metropolitan Development Authority, inside the Hyderabad Metropolitan Region.
3. **DTCP**, the Directorate of Town and Country Planning, in the areas under its own planning control.
4. **The Gram Panchayat or the municipal body**, outside all of the above.

Confirm which one applies before you do anything else, because the authority determines the rules, the forms and the fee. Start from the revenue village, mandal and district of your survey number, and confirm the jurisdiction against the current notification of the authority concerned rather than against a brochure.

## Why an old list of jurisdictions is not reliable

The boundaries that decide the answer have been redrawn more than once recently, and this is where most published guidance has gone stale. G.O.Ms.No.45, dated 05.02.2026, extended GHMC limits into the Core Urban Region and delegated building-permission powers to HMDA with revenue sharing. G.O.Ms.No.55, dated 11.02.2026, constituted three municipal corporations — Cyberabad, Malkajgiri and Greater Hyderabad. G.O.Ms.No.68, dated 12.03.2025, re-declared the Hyderabad Metropolitan Region. A jurisdiction list published before those dates may place your village under an authority that no longer handles it.

Check the current position at the authority's own portal or office, and ask for the position in writing if you are relying on it for a purchase decision.

## The Rudraram case

A plot in Rudraram village, Patancheru mandal, Sangareddy district lies outside GHMC limits, so the GHMC route does not apply to it. The likely route is the Gram Panchayat or the municipal body with jurisdiction over that village, alongside whatever layout approval was granted by HMDA for the layout as a whole.

"Likely" is deliberate. Do not build the route into your plan until you have confirmed it with the local body in writing, and do not assume it is the same body for every plot in a layout. Ask the team for the approval documentation that applies to the layout you are looking at.

## The Act and the Rules you file under

Building permission in Telangana is governed by the Telangana Building (Permission to Construct, Regulated and Progressive Development of Buildings) Act, 2020 — Act 12 of 2020 — together with the Building Rules made under it. The Act brought the state's building regulation into one instrument after the bifurcation, and it is where the definitions, the powers and the procedures sit.

The rules matter as much as the Act. The Building Rules were made by G.O.Ms.No.201 of the Municipal Administration and Urban Development Department, dated 16.11.2020, alongside the Act. The older Telangana Building Rules 2012 (G.O.Ms.No.168, MA&UD, 07.04.2012) still appear in older paperwork, so check which set your file is actually running under. For areas outside the urban bodies, the rules adopted for Gram Panchayats and rural areas are G.O.Ms.No.52 of 12.03.2022, issued jointly by the Panchayat Raj and Rural Development and MA&UD departments.

HMDA works under its own statute, the HMDA Act 2008 (Act 8 of 2008), so an HMDA building permission is a different instrument from a GHMC one even where the form looks similar.

You can check every reference on this page yourself. BuildNow hosts the state's own library of Acts, government orders and memoranda at its [Go and Act page](https://buildnow.telangana.gov.in/go-and-act/), each entry carrying its number, date and subject. If a post — including this one — cites an order you cannot find there, ask us for the reference and we will correct it.

Read the Act and the current Rules from the official Telangana government publications before you rely on any summary, including this one. Procedures under this regime have changed as the portals have changed.

## Filing is not an approval

This is the single most expensive misunderstanding in this market, so it is worth stating without softening: an acknowledgement, a payment receipt, a scrutiny report or an application status is not a building permit. None of those documents authorises you to break ground.

You have a sanctioned building plan when the authority with jurisdiction has issued a sanction order. Before you start construction:

1. Obtain the sanctioned plan with its sanction order and order number.
2. Read it. Note the permitted height, the setbacks, the number of floors, the covered area and any conditions attached.
3. Match the sanction to your survey number and to the plan as it is built on site.
4. Keep the sanction order with the registration documents.

If a seller shows you an application receipt and calls it a permit, ask for the sanction order. If they cannot produce one, the permission does not exist.

"Instant approval", where you see the phrase advertised, applies only to a narrow set of categories and should not be read as a general rule. Ask the authority which categories in your case actually qualify, and insist on the sanction order either way.

## The portal has changed, and bPASS is closing

TG-bPASS, the earlier single-window portal, is being permanently closed after 15 December 2025. BuildNow is the live system for GHMC, HMDA and DTCP building and layout applications. Confirm on the BuildNow portal itself which channel your application runs through before you file or pay.

## What to have ready before you file

The exact document list varies by authority and changes, so take it from the office that will sanction your application. In practice you will be asked for most of the following:

1. Layout approval document and its number, confirmed with the issuing authority.
2. 1B extract and pattadar passbook for the parcel, from the Revenue Department.
3. Registered sale deed and the sub-registrar's record.
4. Land conversion order, if any applies to the parcel — ask whether one is needed before you file.
5. Architectural plan, site plan and any drawings the local body requires.
6. Any NOC the local body lists — road width, drainage, fire and others as applicable.

## What the sanction actually attaches

A sanction order is not a blank cheque. Conditions attached to it normally cover setbacks, the number of floors and permitted height, covered area, the treatment of the setback area, rainwater harvesting, parking provision and construction-waste disposal. Some local bodies also require a commencement intimation before work starts on site.

Read the conditions list as a checklist, because that is what it is. If your drawing departs from a condition, correct it before construction. Keep a copy of the sanction order at the site, since an inspecting officer may ask for it.

Two warnings worth stating plainly. A change made during construction — an extra floor, a larger porch, a change of use — is a new permission, not an adjustment. And a building that departs from the sanctioned plan can be treated as unauthorised construction even where the land is entirely regular, with regularisation a separate process carrying its own conditions and cost.

## Four situations, and what to ask about each

1. **"The layout is approved. Do I still need my own building permission?"** Yes. A layout approval covers the layout; your house on your plot is a separate permission, applied for and sanctioned in your own name. Confirm the requirement with the local body for the specific layout.
2. **"Can I begin on agricultural land and regularise later?"** Do not assume so. What may be built on agricultural land, and whether conversion must come first, is a question for the local body and the Revenue Department — see [land conversion on Telangana plots](/blog/land-conversion-telangana-plots/) for the conversion track itself. Construction without permission does not become lawful by being left alone.
3. **"My builder says the permission is included in the package."** Ask for the document, not the assurance. Ask for the sanction order number and verify it with the issuing authority, following the [parent checklist](/blog/verify-plot-hyderabad/).
4. **"My application has been pending for months."** Pending is not sanctioned. Ask the office for the status in writing, and read any scrutiny observations issued. A file in a queue grants you nothing, and rural layouts have their own separate set of conditions — Memo.No.7740/Plg.III/MAUD/2021, dated 08.06.2021, regulates layouts in Gram Panchayats and addresses unauthorised ones.

- **No village-to-authority list.** The boundaries were redrawn by the orders named above. An outdated list would be worse than none, so confirm the jurisdiction for your survey number at the authority's portal or office.

## On the fee

This post does not publish a building-permission fee, and the widely repeated figure deserves an explicit correction. The ₹5,000 associated with bPASS is a user charge towards correction in an application and in proceedings, imposed by Memo.No.3220/PLG.III/2023 dated 16.05.2023. It is not a permission fee, and quoting it as the cost of a building permit is wrong.

The charges that actually attach to a building permission are set by order and differ by authority, plot area and building type, and they change. Some you may encounter are set out in the state's own library — development charges were revised separately for HMDA, the general urban development authorities and YTDA by orders dated 30.08.2016, and an environmental impact charge on built-up area above 10,000 sq.ft was revised by G.O.Ms.No.17 dated 23.07.2025. We do not publish those amounts here, because a schedule you cannot check against a live order is worth nothing to you. Ask the office that will sanction your plan for the current schedule, and confirm the amount before you pay anyone.
