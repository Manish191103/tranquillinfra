Land described as agricultural cannot be built on because of the land itself. A building permission on an agricultural plot has to rest on a change in permitted use, and in Telangana that change is made by an order under the **Telangana Agricultural Land (Conversion for Non Agricultural purposes) Act, 2006 (Act 3 of 2006)** — not by a seller's assurance, a brochure, or an online form on a property website.

Here is what the Act actually says, who runs the process, which documents prove it happened, and the two separate checks most buyers collapse into one.

## What land conversion is, under which statute

The A.P. Agricultural Land (Conversion for Non Agricultural purposes) Act, 2006 continued in Telangana as Act 3 of 2006, adapted by G.O.Ms.No.4, MA&UD, dated 05.01.2016. The structure of the Act is short and worth reading in full before you argue with a seller about it:

- **Section 3** deals with land use conversion.
- **Section 4** gives the authority power to levy and collect conversion fees and other charges.
- **Section 5** gives it power to make rules.
- **Section 6** bars suits without Government sanction.
- **Section 7** provides the penalty.

The official text is published by the Telangana Law Department and indexed on India Code. Confirm the version in force at the time of your transaction rather than relying on a summary — the Law Department hosts later amendment material as well.

You will also see the older NALA label used loosely in the market. The Hyderabad district administration's service page describes the scheme as having been introduced in 1963, which refers to the original Andhra enactment. The operative Telangana enactment is Act 3 of 2006, so ask for that, not the year.

## Who owns the process, and who does not

**Land conversion belongs to the Revenue Department** — the District Collector, MRO or RDO — and it is applied for through MeeSeva, the state's service portal. The Hyderabad district administration's own service page for land conversion points to the state portal and to the revenue department alone.

A promoter, a builder or an agent cannot convert your land. What a developer can do is apply in the owner's name, or apply for the parcel, or arrange the paperwork — the decision and the order are the department's. That is why the document you need is an order, and not a letter on the developer's letterhead.

For a plotted layout, two tracks run in parallel and are often confused:

1. **Layout approval** from the planning authority — which decides whether the plots are a legitimate layout at all.
2. **Land conversion** — which changes the permitted use of the land.

BuildNow Telangana, the state's application platform, lists "Land Use Certificate" and "Change of Land Use" as their own application types, separate from layout and building approvals. Which planning authority had jurisdiction is covered in [HMDA vs DTCP: which approved your plot?](/blog/hmda-or-dtcp-plot-approval-telangana/), and whether the project is registered with the state regulator is in [RERA registration for projects in Telangana](/blog/rera-telangana-project-registration/).

## The four documents to ask for

Ask the seller for these, in this order, and match every number against the survey number of the plot you are actually being shown:

1. **The pattadar passbook and a current 1B extract** — confirming who is recorded as pattadar. Pull the extract yourself on [Bhu Bharati](/blog/pattadar-passbook-1b-check/) rather than accepting a photocopy.
2. **The current land classification of the land.** Ask the MRO or the Tahasildar for the classification of the survey number on record now, not a classification quoted from years ago.
3. **The conversion order itself** — the land use order, with its number and its date. This is the document that does the work. Ask for the original or a certified copy, not a summary.
4. **Up-to-date land tax receipts** for the period, which show the payee on record against the survey number.

If any of the four cannot be produced, treat that as an open question for the department rather than a detail to be smoothed over.

## The distinction that decides the sale: recorded rights versus classification

**A 1B extract shows recorded rights. It does not show the classification of the land, and a clean 1B is not evidence of conversion.**

This is the single most consequential gap in a plot purchase. A survey number can be recorded in the seller's name, carry a clean pattadar passbook and have no encumbrance, and still be classified agricultural. The two are separate facts maintained in separate systems, and a buyer needs both.

Note also what the statutory basis has become: the **Telangana Bhu Bharati (Record of Rights in Land) Act, 2024** displaces parts of the earlier 1971 record-of-rights law from 12-10-2024, with rules in **Telangana Bhu Bharati (Record of Rights in Land) Rules, 2025**, G.O.Ms.No.39 dated 14.04.2025. If someone tells you the 1971 Act is the current governing statute, they are working from an old note. The wider chain — title, encumbrance, registration — is in the parent checklist: [how to verify a plot in Hyderabad](/blog/verify-plot-hyderabad/).

## "4B" is not an official Telangana document

Some sellers and some property websites hand over a sheet labelled "4B" as proof of conversion. We could not find the term "4B" used for a land conversion or record-of-rights document anywhere in official Telangana revenue or law-department documentation. **Do not rely on it.**

What is confirmed is that "1B" is the standard Record of Rights extract. The term "4B" is most plausibly a confusion with the 1B extract, with some section or rule of a revenue instrument, or a coinage from a private website — we cannot tell which, and that is exactly why it carries no weight. Treat a "4B" as an unlabelled sheet and ask the same question you would ask of anything else: is this the conversion order, and what is its number and date.

## How to check that a conversion order exists

You can test the seller's claim in five steps. Each step has a route you can run yourself.

1. **Get the order number and its date.** A claim of "conversion done" without a number and a date is not a claim you can check.
2. **Ask the department.** Take the survey number, village and mandal to the MRO or Tahasildar, or raise it through MeeSeva, and ask whether a conversion order exists for that survey number and whether its particulars match what you have been shown. Confirm it there before you rely on it.
3. **Check the current classification separately.** Ask for the classification of the survey number as on record now. An order from years earlier may have been overtaken.
4. **Match the numbers, not the words.** Survey number, village, mandal, extent, and the order's own number must agree with the plot in the layout plan.
5. **Have your lawyer read the order.** A lawyer can tell you whether the order covers the parcel, the use you intend, and the holder of the record.

## What this post does not tell you

We do not publish a **conversion fee** figure or a Government Order fixing one. The fee is levied under the Act by the authority, it changes, and we could not confirm a published conversion-fee schedule on an official revenue-department source. Ask the MRO or RDO for the fee that applies to your transaction and the date from which it holds.

We also do not publish a **processing time**. It varies by office and by file, and a number we cannot source would be worse than none. Ask the same office for the current position.

This is general buyer education, not legal advice. Bring your own lawyer to the order and to the layout plan.

Conversion is what makes a layout possible.
