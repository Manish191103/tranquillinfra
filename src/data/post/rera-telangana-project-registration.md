A RERA number is a registration record. Read literally, it says that a project
has been registered with the real estate regulator and that a named set of
disclosures about it is on file. Read loosely, as a badge, it is routinely
mistaken for proof that the land is clean, that the layout is approved and that
your particular plot can be sold to you without a second look. That gap between
the two readings is where plot buyers lose money.

This post is general buyer education on the Real Estate (Regulation and
Development) Act, 2016 and the Telangana Rules made under it. It is not legal
advice. If you are preparing a purchase, take the documents to your own lawyer.

## The law that created the number

The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, Act 16 of 2016, applies
in Telangana. Registration of a project is dealt with in section 5 of the Act,
and the Authority's own order pages cite section 31 alongside Rule 34(1) of the
Telangana Rules. The Rules are the Telangana Real Estate (Regulation and
Development) Rules, 2017, made by G.O.Ms.No.202 of the Municipal Administration
and Urban Development department dated 31.07.2017. The Act text and the Rules
PDF are both published by the government, so you can read the provision
yourself rather than take a summary from a broker.

Registration is not a favour granted to a good builder. It is the statutory
point at which the regulator holds the promoter's disclosures. That is a real
protection, and it is worth understanding on its own terms.

## When a plotted project must register

A plotted project exceeding 500 sq.m of land area is a "project" and must be
registered before any plot in it is advertised, marketed, booked or sold. The
exemption in section 3(2) is for plots of 500 sq.m or less, and up to eight
units. A gated layout of villa plots is comfortably above that floor, which is
why the projects in this corridor carry registration numbers at all.

The test is area and units, not the word "gated", not the presence of a clubhouse
and not the size of the price ticket. If a seller tells you a plot does not need
registration because it is small, ask for the measurement from the sanctioned
plan rather than taking the description.

## What registration actually is

A registration certificate is issued in Form-C, and its language is precise about
what is being granted: registration is granted under section 5 to the project
under a **project registration number**. That is a project number. It is not a
plot number, and it is not a title certification.

Registration means three things, and no more.

1. The project has been registered with the Telangana Real Estate Regulatory
   Authority.
2. The promoter has filed the disclosures the Act and the Rules require — the
   approved layout plan, the land title and the promoter details.
3. Those filings are on the Authority's record.

Registration is a disclosure event. It is not a quality judgement, not a
financial check, not a title investigation and not a promise that the promoter
will finish anything.

Registration is also not the end of the obligation. Section 11 imposes continuing
disclosure duties, and the Authority's own flash notice enforces the quarterly
returns under section 11(1)(b) to (f) for the quarters ending 15/04, 15/07,
15/10 and 15/01. A registration number that sits still while those returns go
unfiled is a warning sign. If a seller wants to talk about compliance, ask which
quarters were filed and check.

An agent's registration under section 9 runs five years and is renewable, and it
covers the agent's own conduct, not your plot.

## What registration does not prove

This is the section worth reading twice, because the failures are quiet.

**It does not prove title to the land.** Registration does not examine who owns
the land, whether the pattadar record matches the promoter, or whether the parcel
carries a mortgage, a litigation claim or a pending land conversion. Those are
established by the 1B extract, the pattadar passbook and the encumbrance
certificate, from the sub-registrar and the revenue office. Read [how to check
the pattadar passbook and the 1B extract](/blog/pattadar-passbook-1b-check/)
and [how to read an encumbrance certificate](/blog/encumbrance-certificate-telangana/)
before relying on either.

**It does not prove the layout approval is valid.** The layout permission is a
separate instrument from the planning authority. For a plot in the Hyderabad
west corridor that authority is usually HMDA, and the number you want is the
HMDA layout permission number, not the RERA number. Confirm it with HMDA and
read [how HMDA and DTCP approvals are issued](/blog/hmda-or-dtcp-plot-approval-telangana/).
Nor does registration say anything about [building
permission](/blog/building-permission-plot-telangana/) once you start building.

**It does not prove that any individual plot is unencumbered, and it does not
substitute for an encumbrance search.** Registration happens at project level.
A specific parcel inside a registered project can still carry a charge, a
litigation notice or a co-owner dispute. Registration does not replace the search
that names your plot.

**It does not make an unregistered plot registrable.** A common and damaging
misreading is that a buyer can regularise a parcel later by pointing at a
registered project nearby. Registration attaches to a project. It does not travel
to a parcel carved out of, sold out of or excluded from it. And the bar on
registering plots by sale in an unapproved layout operates entirely separately,
under the Telangana Regularization of Unapproved and Illegal Layout Rules. [Land
conversion](/blog/land-conversion-telangana-plots/) is a separate question that
registration also does not answer.

**It does not bind you to a price, a payment schedule or a specification.** Those
come from the agreement you sign, and registration does not enforce your
agreement against the promoter for you. It has no bearing on what you pay at
[the sub-registrar](/blog/taxes-on-plot-purchase-telangana/) or on whether a
lender will [finance the plot](/blog/plot-loans-telangana/).

## How to check the number yourself, honestly

Here is the friction nobody advertises. The Telangana public project list is
large — the Authority reported 11,261 registered projects as on 2026-10-01 —
but the search that reaches it is captcha-gated. It needs a "User Type"
selection, then district, mandal and village dropdowns, and only then a captcha
before any query runs. And there is **no permalink that resolves a single
registration number**, so you cannot bookmark one project's record or send a
direct link to your lawyer.

That is why the honest instruction is not "go and search". It is: ask the
promoter for the Form-C registration certificate and match the number printed on
that document.

1. Ask for the certificate itself, or a certified copy. Not a brochure page, not
   a screenshot of a search result.
2. Read the project registration number off it, character for character.
3. If you want to confirm it against the Authority's own record, go to the
   public project search, solve the captcha, and select the district, mandal and
   village from the dropdowns rather than typing them in. For a Rudraram village
   project that is Sangareddy district, Patancheru mandal, Rudraram village.
4. Read the project name, the promoter name and the number on the record, and
   compare.

If the numbers do not match, or the project does not appear where you expect it,
stop and ask. A promoter who cannot produce the certificate is telling you
something.

### On the shape of the number

There is no published format rule for Telangana registration numbers. Three
shapes are seen in practice: a "P" prefix followed by eleven digits, an "A"
prefix followed by eleven digits, and the "REA0" form followed by eight digits.
The REA prefix is a real Telangana prefix, and it is visible on the Authority's
own published defaulters list, which carries REA-prefixed project numbers for
named projects and promoters — REA02200025418 and REA02400083841 were both
listed there when we read it on 1 October 2026. Whether the REA form denotes a
distinct class of project is not something we can confirm from any published
rule, so we do not assert it. Read the number exactly as the certificate prints
it.

## What registration requires the promoter to disclose

The Act requires the promoter to disclose the approved layout plan, the land
title and the promoter details. In practice, request each as an original
instrument or a certified copy:

1. The approved layout plan — the drawing as sanctioned, with the plot numbering.
2. The land title — the documents establishing the promoter's claim, which you
   check against the 1B extract and the encumbrance certificate, not against the
   promoter.
3. Promoter details — the entity, its address and its contact details.

Cross-check each at its own source: the layout approval with the planning
authority, the land records with the sub-registrar and the revenue office, and
the RERA filings with the Authority. A document set is only as good as the
weakest item you have not independently checked.

For our own project, the disclosures behind REA01100108192 sit with the HMDA
layout permission 2103/HMDA/SWDL/2026 and with the layout described on [the
Tranquill City page](/projects/tranquill-city/). Read the numbers from the
documents, not from this site.

## What we do not publish here, and why

We do not publish a RERA registration fee or a statutory processing time. Those
figures are set by the Act, the Rules and a fee schedule that is amended by
government order, and publishing a figure we cannot cite to a current primary
instrument is exactly the habit this site exists to avoid. Ask the Telangana RERA
Authority for the fee and the current timeline.

We also do not publish a possession date, a development-completion date or a
per-band availability count for any project, ours included. A date we publish is
a date we would be held to, and an availability count goes stale within a day.
Ask the team for the current price of the plots you are comparing.

## If something does not match

The Authority operates a separate complaints portal, and using it is a real right
of a buyer or a prospective buyer, not a favour. It reported 2,958 complainants
as on 2026-10-01.

Raise a complaint where the information on the Authority's record does not match
what you were told, or where you believe the project is being marketed in a
manner the Act or the Rules do not permit. Before you file, gather the
registration number, the certificate, the documents you were given, and a note of
what differs and where you obtained each one. A complaint with documents moves;
an impression does not. Keep copies of everything you submit.
