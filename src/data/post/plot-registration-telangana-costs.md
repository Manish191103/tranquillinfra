Registration is the step where a plot purchase becomes a public record — and the point where the money you pay to the government is decided by two things: the **heads of charge** and the **value** they are computed on. Both are published by the department, and both surprise buyers who budgeted on the quoted price alone.

> **Reader note.** This is general buyer education, not legal advice. What a registration costs depends on the instrument, the value fixed under the Act, and the local body limits of the property. Get the computation for your own plot from the sub-registrar.
>
> Last checked against the Registration & Stamps Department's current Ready Reckoner of stamp duty rates (which carries the G.O.Ms.No.59 revision of 20 July 2021) and the Table of Registration Fees effective 2 September 2021, plus the department's stamp duty and registration FAQs, on 1 October 2026.

## The charges on a sale deed

Four lines appear in a registration computation, and the three duty lines come to **7.5%** of the value in total:

- **Stamp duty.** In the department's current schedule, a sale deed attracts **5.5%** (Article 47-A of Schedule I-A, revised by G.O.Ms.No.59 dated 20 July 2021 and effective from 22 July 2021).
- **Transfer duty.** Charged only on property within municipal or urban limits — **1.5%** where the property falls outside a Gram Panchayat area, and **0%** inside one. It is shown as its own line on the sub-registrar's computation.
- **Registration fee.** For sale deeds, **2%** of value for property inside Gram Panchayat areas and **0.5%** for property outside them, under the Table of Registration Fees effective **2 September 2021** — notified in the Telangana Gazette on that date under Section 78 of the Registration Act, 1908, and communicated by the departmental letter C&IG(R&S) Lr.No. G3/5334/2021 dated 12 August 2021. That table superseded **both G.O.Ms.No.60 of 20 July 2021** (itself effective 22 July 2021) and **G.O.Ms.No.463 of 17 August 2013**. If you see the fee split attributed to either of those orders, you are reading a superseded instrument.
- **User charges**, which vary with the service used.

The two fee lines are set against each other. The split moves with the local body limits of the plot; the total does not. **5.5% + 1.5% + 0.5% = 7.5%**, and **5.5% + 0% + 2% = 7.5%**. That is worth knowing in both directions. A buyer who assumes transfer duty is always payable can over-budget; a buyer who treats it as never payable _and_ takes the 0.5% fee on a village plot computes 6% and under-budgets by **₹75,000 on a ₹50 lakh plot**. If a computation you are shown has one of the two percentages missing, ask for it.

Worked example — a plot at **₹50 lakh** whose government market value is not higher: ₹2,75,000 stamp duty, ₹0 transfer duty inside a Gram Panchayat area, and ₹1,00,000 registration fee, so **₹3,75,000, or 7.5%**, before user charges. Every one of those percentages runs on the value fixed under Section 47-A, so a higher market value raises all three lines. There is **no cap and no minimum** on the registration fee for a sale deed: the department's order and its current Ready Reckoner both print a bare percentage for that row, and the rows immediately above and below it carry the minimum and maximum limits where the schedule intends any. If you have read that a sale deed's registration fee is capped, that figure belongs to the **agreement and GPA** instruments, not to the deed — see below.

The schedule is worth reading before you sign anything, because it prices different instruments differently, and getting this wrong costs real money. **An agreement to sell comes in two versions, and the difference is not small.** An agreement **without possession** carries 0.5%, and that payment is not adjustable against the duty on the sale deed. An agreement **with possession** carries the full **5.5%** — and that one *is* credited against the duty on the sale deed you go on to execute. On a ₹50 lakh plot the gap between the two is ₹2,50,000 of budget: pay the 0.5% when possession has already changed hands and you are out by five percent of the value. Which version you are signing is not a formality; ask for the agreement in writing before the token, and ask which column of the schedule it sits in. The agreement-cum-GPA is priced differently again — the department's own tables currently disagree with each other on that row, so take the figure from the sub-registrar's computation rather than from any published table, ours included.

## The value the charges are computed on

The charges are not calculated on whatever figure the parties agree to write. Under **Section 47-A** of the Indian Stamp Act, the value taken is the **higher of the sale consideration or the government's market value** for that property. The market value is fixed by committees from the registration, revenue and local bodies, published as the basic value registers, and searchable on the department's own market-value service.

### The guideline values moved in June 2026

This is the part that has changed since this post was first published, and it changes the rupee figure without changing a single percentage. Telangana revised market and guideline values with effect from **5 June 2026**, in slabs of **25%, 50%, 75% or 100%** over the existing registration values, for agricultural and non-agricultural land alike. The 100% slab is reserved for ORR-adjacent, highway, radial-road and upmarket core-urban pockets. Roughly 10% of Sub-Registrar Office areas were left unchanged.

One thing to be clear about, because the number does not exist: **no G.O. or notification number has been issued for this revision.** It was announced through a Revenue Ministry press statement carried on 4 June 2026, alongside Sub-Registrar Office field-survey reports, and that is how it is cited here. If a seller or an intermediary quotes you a G.O. number for the 2026 guideline values, treat it as unverified until the department confirms it.

Why it matters to your bill: because all three duty lines run on the higher of consideration and guideline value, a 25 to 100% uplift in the guideline value raises the amount you hand to the sub-registrar **even though the percentages did not move**. The 7.5% total is unchanged. What changed is the base it is applied to. A worked example built on the quoted price alone can therefore be materially short, and the only reliable figure for your plot is the sub-registrar's computation for that survey number.

If the parties dispute the notified value, the sub-registrar keeps the document pending and refers the question to the District Registrar after collecting 50% of the deficit duty as a deposit. The District Registrar hears the parties, may inspect the spot, and passes an order — appealable to the civil court within 60 days.

## The clock — four months, not four weeks

A sale deed must be presented for registration **within four months of execution**. If that window is missed, it can be presented in the following four months with a penalty of up to ten times the registration fee, if the District Registrar grants permission. Beyond eight months, the document cannot be accepted for registration at all.

Registering late is not a formality you can fix at leisure. It is the difference between holding a registered title and holding a dispute.

## What cannot be registered

Section 22-A of the Registration Act prohibits registration of documents for land that is alienated in breach of statute, government land sold by people not empowered to sell it, land declared surplus, and other categories the State notifies. The department publishes a searchable list of prohibited properties — check it before you negotiate, not after.

One more category matters for plots specifically: **plots in unapproved layouts cannot be registered by sale**. If the layout was never validly approved, there is nothing for the sub-registrar to register until it is regularized. The [approvals guide](/blog/hmda-or-dtcp-plot-approval-telangana/) explains how to check which authority should have approved the layout you are being shown. It also decides the finance: a lender's security is a registered plot, which is the chain [plot loans in Telangana](/blog/plot-loans-telangana/) follows.

## The encumbrance certificate

An **encumbrance certificate (EC)** lists the transactions registered against a property — which is how mortgages, prior sales and registered liabilities surface. What it does not list is anything unregistered, and the department is explicit that an EC cannot be taken as the sole criterion for a purchase. It is one input into title review, not a replacement for it.

Practical details: an EC costs Rs.500 per certificate for a search up to 30 years and Rs.1,000 for longer periods, under the Table of Registration Fees effective 2 September 2021. The department's FAQ still says certificates are issued through MeeSeva centres and sub-registrar or district-registrar offices, but circulars G1/13559/2012 dated 24.07.2012 and Government Memo 29588/Regn-I/A1/2012-3 dated 06.07.2013 stopped EC and certified-copy issue from Sub-Registrar offices in the remaining districts and moved it to MeeSeva, and the FAQ text appears not to have been revised. **Plan around MeeSeva, and confirm by telephone whether your Sub-Registrar office still issues one before you travel** rather than assuming either. The fee table does not say whether MeeSeva adds a service charge, so ask at the centre and pay what is receipted. The full treatment, including the three search routes and what a nil EC does not prove, is in [encumbrance certificates in Telangana](/blog/encumbrance-certificate-telangana/).

And a warning that appears in the department's own FAQ: a General Power of Attorney **does not transfer ownership**. Deals that run through a GPA and stop there leave the title with the seller, regardless of what was paid.

## Before you pay

- Ask the sub-registrar's office for the **computation slip** for that plot, and compare it with the department's calculator.
- Match the deed to the plot: survey number, village, mandal, extent, and the seller named on the document.
- Get the **EC** for a period your lawyer recommends.
- Have your lawyer read the title chain, not just the current deed.
- Confirm the project's **RERA registration** if it applies, and keep the approval documentation with your papers.
- Keep every challan, e-stamp receipt and user-charge receipt — these are what establish that duty was paid.
