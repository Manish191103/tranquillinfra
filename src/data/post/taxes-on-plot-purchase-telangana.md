Buying a plot in Telangana puts three different tax charges on you: charges collected
at the sub-registrar's office when you register, tax deducted at source when you pay,
and tax again if you sell. Three different bodies collect them and three different
places confirm them. Conflating them is expensive, so this post separates them.

One thing to know before you read further. The Income-tax Act, 1961 was repealed on
1 April 2026 and replaced by the Income-tax Act, 2025. The 1961 Act stands repealed
by section 536 of the 2025 Act. Read that section in the Act as enacted if you want
to see the repeal for yourself. The section numbers were reorganised in the process.
Much of the Indian content online, including material published this year, still
quotes the 1961 numbering, and some of it is confidently wrong.

## What to do first

1. Confirm which section of the Income-tax Act, 2025 governs TDS on a property
   purchase today. Read the Act as enacted on the Income Tax Department portal.
2. Check both the consideration and the stamp duty value of this plot against the
   Rs. 50 lakh threshold, and ask your tax adviser how other purchases in this tax
   year are treated.
3. Register the purchase at the sub-registrar and collect the stamped receipt. Stamp
   duty and registration fee for Telangana are set out in the
   [registration post](/blog/plot-registration-telangana-costs/), with the
   government order references and the verification route for each figure.
4. Establish which local body has jurisdiction over the revenue village before you
   budget any land tax.
5. If a sale is inside your planning horizon, read the capital gains section below
   before you buy, not after.

## TDS when you buy: section 393 of the 2025 Act

The buyer's TDS obligation on a property purchase is now under section 393(1) of the
Income-tax Act, 2025, at the Table entry for transfer of immovable property other
than agricultural land, payable by the person buying.

This was section 194-IA of the Income-tax Act, 1961 before 1 April 2026. If a
chartered accountant, a lawyer or a property intermediary shows you 194-IA, they
are quoting the repealed Act. That is not necessarily bad faith, because the change is
recent and much of the professional literature has not caught up. But it does mean the
document in front of you was prepared against a repealed statute. Ask which Act they
are working from, and ask to see the corresponding provision in the 2025 Act.

The rate is 1%, and the base is not simply the price on the agreement. Read the
provision: tax is deducted at 1% of the consideration or the stamp duty value of the
property, whichever is higher. Rural agricultural land is outside this provision.

The threshold is Rs. 50 lakh, and the provision is framed per property: no deduction
arises where both the consideration and the stamp duty value of that property are
below the threshold. How several purchases in one tax year are then treated is a
question for your tax adviser, and you should confirm the reading before you rely on
it. Where both limbs are below the threshold, no payment is due, but the reporting
duty stands: the purchase is still recorded so the credit can be claimed. Note that
the guideline value and the agreement price do not always agree, and it is the higher
of the two that counts. Confirm the threshold, the base and the current rate in
section 393 on the Income Tax Department portal, and confirm the filed
challan-cum-statement with the sub-registrar who records the transaction.

The form changed too, and this is where most advice is now out of date. Transactions
up to 31 March 2026 were reported on Form 26QB. For transactions on or after 1 April
2026, the challan-cum-statement for deduction under section 393(1) is filed on Form
141, a consolidated form that replaced Forms 26QB, 26QC, 26QD and 26QE. The Forms
and Downloads section of the Income Tax Department portal carries the current form
and its note. Check which form your transaction date falls under.

The buyer's practical position: TDS is not an extra cost. It is your advance tax,
credited to your own income tax account and adjustable against your liability. Before
you pay your income tax for the year, check your refund statement to confirm the
credit has actually appeared. If it has not, raise it with your sub-registrar and the
seller together, because the statement links both PAN numbers.

Checklist for the TDS step:

- Check the consideration and the stamp duty value of the plot against the Rs. 50 lakh
  threshold.
- Where the threshold is met, deduct 1% of the higher of the two at the time of
  payment or credit, whichever is earlier.
- Ensure both buyer and seller are recorded with correct PAN numbers.
- Collect the filed challan-cum-statement, on Form 141 for a transaction dated on or
  after 1 April 2026.
- Verify the credit in your income tax refund statement before you claim it.
- Read section 393 in the Act as enacted, because the numbering changed on 1 April 2026.

We publish no rate other than the one above, and no due dates, because both change
with amendments. Confirm each figure on the portal before you rely on it.

## Capital gains when you sell: section 72 of the 2025 Act

Selling a plot is a capital transaction. The mode of computation of capital gains
under the Income-tax Act, 2025 is section 72. Read it in the Act as enacted before you
file.

Two corrections worth making explicitly, because both circulate widely.

First, a widely repeated claim online states that capital gains under the new Act is
section 123. That is wrong. Section 123 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 is a deduction
for life insurance premia, deferred annuity and provident fund contributions. If a
return or a computation is prepared on the strength of that section number, have it
reviewed before it is filed.

Second, if you see section 48 cited as the new capital gains computation, check which
Act is being discussed. Section 48 of the 1961 Act was the computation provision and
section 72 of the 2025 Act replaces it, but section 48 of the 2025 Act is a different
provision altogether, on tea, coffee and rubber development accounts. Section 72 is
the one to read.

What this post does not do is give you the holding period that separates a short-term
from a long-term asset. The period, and the section that fixes it, are not something
we will paraphrase from memory in a buyer-education post, and we will not publish a
section number we have not read in the Act as enacted. The Central Board of Direct
Taxes publishes guidance on the classification of capital assets on its portal; read
it alongside the 2025 Act before you fix a purchase date around a sale date.

Two points that are stable across the change:

1. The holding period, not the buyer's intent, decides the character of the gain.
   Buying a plot with a firm plan to sell is still a capital transaction.
2. The computation in section 72 deducts expenditure incurred wholly and exclusively
   in connection with the transfer, together with the cost of acquisition and the cost
   of any improvement. Keep the stamped receipt from the sub-registrar, because your
   cost of acquisition depends on it, and keep your improvement invoices.

Buying on loan changes the arithmetic of the gain, because the interest is a cost of
holding rather than a cost of purchase. Section 72 also expressly disallows a set of
items as deductions in computing capital gains, so read the section rather than
assuming your deduction list carried over from the old Act. The
[plot loans post](/blog/plot-loans-telangana/) sets out how bank finance on land
purchase is structured, and is worth reading before you fix an exit date.

## Land tax: check jurisdiction before you budget

This is where buyers on the Hyderabad west corridor most often go wrong, and the error
is a simple one. GHMC property tax and GHMC Vacant Land Tax are instruments of the
Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation. They apply within GHMC limits, which is why
the GHMC property tax page asks you for a PTIN and the vacant land tax search asks for
a ward and assessment number.

Rudraram is a village in Patancheru mandal, Sangareddy district. It sits outside GHMC
limits. GHMC property tax and GHMC Vacant Land Tax most likely do not attach to a plot
there. Confirm the limits against your survey number on the GHMC portal, and confirm
with the Gram Panchayat office of the revenue village where the plot sits.

The local body with jurisdiction will tell you whether any land tax or vacant land tax
demand stands against the survey number. Rates are set by that body and are revised, so
no rate is published here. Ask for the current demand notice, or a no-demand
certificate, for that specific survey number. The
[Sangareddy district administration site](https://sangareddy.telangana.gov.in/)
carries the revenue office contacts you need.

How such a charge is transferred is usually dealt with in the sale deed and through a
no-objection certificate. Ask the seller for a no-objection certificate or a
tax-receipt history for the survey number, and read what the deed says about
outstanding charges. Whether that certificate is sufficient, or whether you should
apply separately in your own name, is a question for your lawyer at the point of
registration.

An unpaid land tax is a charge on the land, so it belongs in the record search before
you sign. The [encumbrance certificate post](/blog/encumbrance-certificate-telangana/)
sets out how to check the sub-registrar's record and what the certificate does and
does not prove.

## What is not published here, and why

- No stamp duty or registration fee. Those figures are set by the state and revised by
  government order, so they belong in the
  [registration post](/blog/plot-registration-telangana-costs/) with their order
  references rather than here, where they would drift out of date.
- No capital gains holding period, and no section number for it. We do not paraphrase
  statutory text we have not read in the Act as enacted.
- No processing time for the TDS challan-cum-statement. It varies with the volume at the
  office and changes without notice.
- No land tax rate, because the rate belongs to the local body with jurisdiction and
  is revised by that body.
- No advance tax figures or due dates, because they are notified annually and are on the
  Income Tax Department portal.
